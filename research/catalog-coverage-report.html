Background

The catalog's market is the United States and its core identity is make/model/model year. Its 4,672 bundled entries combine EPA all-years data, six annual EPA guide files, selected historical supplements, and a small vPIC supplement. Separate development records add exact trim/configuration previews. The CSV audit keeps these evidence types separate; a model-year listing is not a verified retail trim, and an era membership is not a precise vehicle configuration. [EPA's download description] 1 [NHTSA's stated vPIC scope] 3 [EPA's guide limitations] 4

The sources answer different questions. EPA data and annual guides establish that a fuel-economy record with a particular model/year label exists. NHTSA vPIC reports manufacturer-submitted information and is designed around vehicle identification. Neither source promises an exhaustive retail-sales census; the EPA testing archive and guides also document program-specific bounds and exclusions. Therefore a source match supports the identity label in that system, not proof that every U.S. retail offering is included or that an absent vehicle was not sold. 1 3 4 5

The work stayed in the development environment. The inventory is frozen as research evidence rather than used to alter the Browse catalog: source records, returned option rows, unique profile IDs, and detailed trim previews are separate exports. This lets a future review identify a changed source or a new option without treating it as a new vehicle or silently overwriting prior source evidence. Coverage verification · Source inventory

4. Historical model-year transitions need direct, year-specific evidence

Calendar production dates and model years are not interchangeable. U.S. regulation defines model year from the manufacturer's annual production period; GM's announcement that final Camaro assembly was expected in January 2024 while identifying the final year as MY2024 is a concrete example. The project should retain source model-year labels rather than derive them from a plant date. 16 17 4

Ford and Lincoln records show why an apparent year gap should not be automatically filled or labeled a discontinuation. The Henry Ford describes surviving Lincoln production records for limited year ranges and warns that the record series is incomplete; the National WWII Museum and MotorCities describe the 1942 civilian-production interruption from different historical perspectives. Together they support a major production disruption, but do not prove the retail model-year status of every Ford or Lincoln nameplate in 1943–45. Lincoln's Town Car page places the nameplate's 1981 release and later generation spans in a retrospective, a useful distinction from its Continental lineage but not a year-by-year sales census. 15 18 19 20

The Dodge/Ram example requires similar care. A 2009 report documents the announcement of the Ram/Dodge truck-brand split; the project's EPA-linked records and vPIC's manufacturer-reported make/model layer preserve their own names and year labels. Announcement year, first stand-alone model year, corporate brand organization, and the source label attached to a specific model-year record are different facts. The project should preserve those exact source labels and avoid a single retroactive year rule. 21 1 3 22

The EPA's separate MY1990 Chrysler index lists Chrysler-branded models such as LeBaron, New Yorker, and Town and Country for that year. Its year-specific label is evidence about the 1990 index, not a rule for mapping later corporate or brand changes backward. 23 1 22

For the newer EV makes, the exact government listings support Tesla/Roadster in MY2008, Rivian/R1T and R1S in MY2022, and Lucid/Air in MY2022. Tesla's contemporary release calls the Roadster a 2008 model-year vehicle; the Rivian and Lucid comparisons use exact NHTSA make IDs and model-year endpoints. These results validate the cited entries and help separate model year from first production or delivery, but do not prove the earliest year sold to U.S. customers. 24 9 25 26 27 28

5. MY2027 entries require an entry-by-entry status, not a blanket assumption

EPA/FuelEconomy.gov marks the MY2027 data as preliminary. Current vPIC queries return Tesla, Rivian, and Lucid model/year listings for MY2027, and official Ford/Lincoln pages also carry some 2027 labels. Those are useful corroborating records for their stated scope, but an EPA or vPIC listing does not itself establish final specifications, sale, delivery, or a complete retail lineup. The catalog's preliminary flag should remain unless an exact year-specific source resolves that entry; current official lineup pages are manufacturer statements, not full retail-market censuses. 1 29 30 31 32 33

EPA's Rivian MY2026 certification application is additional evidence for EPA-listed carlines, not proof of customer delivery. Current vPIC make lists report Tesla Make_ID 441, Rivian 10887, and Lucid 10919, but those current IDs do not reconstruct historical assignment or establish sale status. Use each result for the field it actually records. 34 25 27 35 29

6. Evidence links, source terms, and release are separate gates

The 43 trim/configuration previews are development-preview identities with accepted evidence claims in the database export. Their evidence table links 25 distinct source URLs. This is a stronger trace than an unsupported trim string, but it does not show that every source page or PDF was checked for the exact market/year/body/powertrain crosswalk, or that all terms for automated access, retention, attribution, and display have been reviewed. Trim evidence CSV Profile audit

The internal source inventory marks the bundled records allowed, but that is a project metadata value rather than a source-by-source legal conclusion. EPA's general license says EPA-produced data are public domain by default unless otherwise specified and directs users to dataset-specific metadata; it does not settle the origin and terms of every field in FuelEconomy.gov files. NHTSA's API Use Policy addresses bulk VIN lookup, while the reviewed project queries use exact model/year listing endpoints. The policy should be applied to its stated scope—not treated as either a blanket ban or an affirmative license for all retained vPIC data. 36 1 37 3 7

For historical Wikipedia-derived material, CC BY-SA 4.0 has attribution terms and provisions for qualifying substantial database-rights reuse. The license text does not decide whether this normalized factual catalog uses enough licensed expression or protected database contents to trigger a particular obligation. Assess the source, access method, material actually retained, and the collection's selection/arrangement; do not presume facts about a manufacturer require that manufacturer's permission, and do not copy source prose, tables, or images. 38 36 37

Analysis

The catalog is substantially more traceable than the first snapshot count suggested. The earlier 146-profile discrepancy was a counting problem: 43 exact-trim profiles were present in database-backed details, and 103 more option rows reflected overlapping generation memberships. Once profile identity is separated from option rows, the totals reconcile without discarding the overlapping candidates. The row-level checks then establish that the EPA IDs and model-year guides map back to their source material; the remaining uncertainty is not primarily the existence of those source rows, but what those source systems can and cannot say about the broader U.S. market.

That distinction matters most before 1984. EPA's available older records are organized around testing and fuel-economy guides, and the selected historical records fill only some names and years. The federal and corporate archives found in this research describe bounded collections; they do not provide a common, all-make, year-by-year U.S. sales list. Consequently, the 660 within-name gaps should remain research leads. Even a correctly matching secondary source row does not prove every adjacent year, and the absence of an EPA or vPIC row does not establish that a model was never sold.

Future years have a different risk. A source may record a model-year label before final retail details are established; a manufacturer's current page may advance its labels over time; and a current vPIC result can reflect a manufacturer's submission without proving delivery. Per-entry review should preserve the evidence date and source field, keep MY2027 provisional where the evidence is provisional, and distinguish identity from final U.S. availability. The regulatory model-year definition and the Ford/GM transition examples show why calendar production dates and nameplate continuity cannot replace year-specific evidence.

The 43 exact-trim previews also need a separate posture. Their claims and links give reviewers a starting point, but “accepted” is an internal research status, not an independent assurance of all package predicates, a total trim census, or public reuse permission. Keep those profiles in the preview tier until exact configuration relationships are independently checked. Source-specific terms should be evaluated narrowly: ordinary factual vehicle attributes are not the same thing as copied brochures, copyrighted prose, images, or protected data presentation.

Limitations

This is a source audit, not a reconstruction of all U.S. vehicle sales. The 539 secondary historical identities have not each been corroborated against a complete archive; the current EPA file does not reproduce the stored ZIP checksum; and all 660 year-gap leads remain unadjudicated. EPA and vPIC coverage is not a retail-sales census. The detailed trim sources have not all received independent page-level content and terms review. The report does not prove actual production, U.S. dealer availability, sale, delivery, or configuration from an EPA/vPIC listing alone. The current preliminary status for MY2027 must be reviewed entry by entry before any public-facing claim. The focused searches found no exhaustive source covering all 13 makes before 1984. 1 3 5 13 15

Recommendations

  1. Keep the catalog private and unreleased. The current evidence does not justify a complete-coverage claim, and no publication is authorized.
  2. Retain the verified CSV audit as the review baseline. The profile export includes the 4,715 unique IDs, each static source locator, its saved hash, and the cross-check result. Keep the 4,818-option export beside it so future revisions do not merge profile counts with era-option counts.
  3. Preserve the EPA source-version discrepancy. Re-fetch the exact file when needed, record the retrieval date and checksum, and compare source revisions before describing future checks as snapshot-identical.
  4. Treat the 660 year gaps as an unresolved worklist. Resolve only with direct model-year evidence; record legitimate hiatuses, name changes, and known source limitations separately. Do not fill years through interpolation.
  5. Keep model-year and exact-trim evidence in separate review queues. The trim previews need independent exact-configuration crosswalks; model-year profiles need historical source verification where the source is secondary.
  6. Review reuse terms source by source. Confirm the actual dataset, endpoint, fields retained, method of access, attribution, caching, and any public display conditions. Apply restrictions to the evidence source that carries them rather than requiring blanket OEM permission for factual specifications.
  7. Keep preliminary year labels visible in internal review and verify each profile before publication. EPA/vPIC/OEM presence should not be presented as a guarantee of finalized sale or delivery.
