# Ford and Lincoln U.S. model-year continuity research

**Status:** private research; no production/catalog changes made. Public release
remains blocked pending explicit owner approval.

## Key Facts

- The reconciled development Browse census is 4,818 selectable option rows
  across 4,715 unique profile IDs: 4,672 model-year identities plus 43
  trim/configuration previews. The 103 extra option rows are additional
  generation/refresh memberships on 102 model-year IDs. The 879 Ford and 237
  Lincoln bundled model-year identities are source-linked in the profile
  audit. EPA, guide and vPIC record matching is summarized in
  `research/catalog-source-verification.md`; selected pre-1978 identities and
  historical completeness remain open.
- Read-only static tally of the current bundled model-year JSON: **879 Ford
  rows across 94 displayed model labels and 237 Lincoln rows across 25 labels**
  (1,116 combined). Ford model-year rows range 1928–2027; Lincoln rows range
  1940–2027. These are counts of bundle identities, not proof of sales,
  completeness, or retail trims.
- Before 1984, the bundle has **195 Ford** and **53 Lincoln** identities.
  Historical coverage is sparse/selective rather than a continuous brand
  chronology. Ford has bundled year rows in 1928–31 and 1955–77 for selected
  families, and EPA-guide-sourced identities in 1978–83. Lincoln includes
  limited 1940–42/1946–48 Continental entries; selected 1952–60 models; and
  selected 1978–83 lines. The first years in these sequences are not brand
  launch dates.
- The Henry Ford archive states that its surviving Lincoln production records
  cover model years 1940–42 and 1946–48, and explicitly warns that its runs are
  incomplete. It also identifies Lincoln Model K records from 1936–39 and
  surviving Model L/KB ledgers from earlier years. This is valuable evidence
  of source availability, not a comprehensive list of every Lincoln sold or
  proof that omitted years had no U.S. model.
- Wartime: the National WWII Museum states that Ford's nonmilitary car and
  truck operations ceased on **February 10, 1942**, after about 160,000
  civilian vehicles had been built in 1942. MotorCities separately reports
  Lincoln/Mercury engine production ceased January 28, 1942; automotive plants
  shifted to war production February 10; and the last Lincoln off the line was
  a 1942 Zephyr. The Henry Ford surviving Lincoln record ranges resume at MY
  1946 after MY1942. Together these sources support a substantial civilian
  production interruption with a documented Lincoln record gap for 1943–45;
  they do not independently prove every Ford/Lincoln retail model-year boundary
  or rule out exceptional government-authorized vehicles. Calendar production
  dates must not be treated as model years.
- Nameplate identity cannot be inferred across similar labels or gaps. Bundled
  examples that require separate treatment include:
  - Ford Bronco 1984–96 and 2021–26, versus separate pre-1984 `BRONCO 4WD`
    EPA-guide labels in 1981–83.
  - Ford Mustang classic supplement entries for 1965–77, `MUSTANG II` in
    1978, and EPA-guide `MUSTANG` rows for 1979–83.
  - Lincoln Aviator 2003–05 and 2020–27, with no continuity inferred across
    2006–19.
  - Lincoln Continental's separate recorded periods 1940–42, 1946–48,
    1958–77, 1980/82–83 guide labels, and 1984–2020 entries. The Mark-series
    and Continental names must not be silently merged.
  - Lincoln Town Car's EPA guide label `TOWN CAR` for 1981–83 and `Town Car`
    1984–2011. Lincoln's own page says it became its own line for MY1981 and
    separately describes model-year generations; this is better identity
    evidence than the page headline's overall production dates.
- The bundled static catalog currently lists MY2026 for 10 Ford and 6 Lincoln
  displayed models, and MY2027 for two Ford models (Expedition and Explorer)
  and two Lincoln models (Aviator and Navigator). Search evidence from Ford.com
  explicitly names the 2027 Explorer; in this pass I did not find comparable
  Ford-published 2027 Expedition evidence. Lincoln.com names four MY2026 SUVs
  in its FAQ, while the same live page labels `Corsair Hybrid`, Aviator and
  Navigator as MY2027. Its 2027 Corsair Hybrid lead is not represented as a
  MY2027 row in this bundle. OEM publication is evidence of an announced
  model-year identity, not a complete verified U.S. sales roster.
- The bundle's pre-1978 classic supplement attributes its selective Ford and
  Lincoln name/year identities to Wikipedia contributors (CC BY-SA 4.0) in
  its source metadata. This pass did not fetch those pages or rely on them as
  independent historical verification. Their recorded normalized-fact
  permission does not replace primary/archival cross-reference or separate
  approval of any later public distribution.
- The EPA-derived catalog source itself flags MY2027 listings as potentially
  preliminary. The 2027 bundle rows should therefore remain explicitly
  preliminary/under review; they must not be upgraded to verified retail
  availability merely because an EPA/OEM page contains a year label.
- Source limitations matter: EPA fuel-economy rows/groups establish that the
  cited EPA record uses a make/model/year label; they do not establish every
  retail trim, actual production or U.S. dealer availability. NHTSA omissions
  likewise do not establish non-sale. Public source access does not grant
  commercial storage or redistribution rights.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

- **WWII interruption and restart year:** Museum/heritage reporting and the
  archive's surviving record ranges are consistent with an interruption after
  1942 and a Lincoln MY1946 return. The exact first postwar retail model-year
  chronology for every Ford and Lincoln series needs model-specific primary
  production/order records. Missing EPA/NHTSA entries are not proof of
  non-sale.
- **Lincoln Town Car boundary:** Lincoln's own history says it became a
  separate line in MY1981, while its page's general intro calls it produced
  1980–2011. Preserve the distinction between calendar-year production and
  model year; don't use 1980 as a Town Car model year based on that headline.
- **Continental succession/name changes:** The bundled identities show gaps
  and distinct Continental/Mark names. Henry Ford archival record coverage is
  incomplete; no transitive continuity should be inferred from similar names.
- **MY2027 records:** Ford search evidence names a 2027 Explorer, but the
  bundle's 2027 Expedition row lacks Ford-published corroboration in this pass.
  Lincoln's official live roster carries MY2027 Aviator, Navigator and Corsair
  Hybrid, while the bundle includes only Aviator/Navigator. Confirm
  model-specific U.S. order guides or fixed EPA records and record
  retrieval/version dates before treating any preliminary record as final.
- **Source consolidation:** Current normalized Ford/Lincoln model-year rows
  blend EPA 1984-present records, EPA 1978–83 guide labels and a selective
  classic supplement attributed to Wikipedia contributors. Shared
  make/model normalization can conceal source label changes; no interpolation
  should bridge a year/name gap.

## Source Quality Assessment

- **Tier 1 — U.S. government primary source:** EPA FuelEconomy.gov all-years
  data and 1978–83 Fuel Economy Guides. These support source-listed
  make/model/year identities within their stated scope. The reviewed catalog
  lists its EPA source as rights-allowed for normalized facts, and explicitly
  limits the entries: EPA group labels are not retail trims, production
  evidence, or a full sales roster; MY2027 may be preliminary.
- **Tier 1 — independent archival institution:** The Henry Ford's production
  records/collection pages describe actual surviving source record ranges and
  their limitations. A record range is not an exhaustive production
  chronology. Its data is especially useful for Lincoln pre-1984 history.
- **Tier 1 — museum history:** The National WWII Museum's 2022 article gives a
  dated account of Ford civilian production cessation. This is a secondary
  historical narrative, not a Ford/Lincoln serial ledger.
- **Tier 2 — regional heritage organization:** MotorCities' 2023 account
  supplies the Lincoln/Mercury January 28 and February 10, 1942 milestones and
  names the last Lincoln as a 1942 Zephyr. Cross-check before turning this into
  a complete model-year gap rule.
- **Tier 3 — manufacturer primary for self-reported product facts:** Ford and
  Lincoln's model/history pages are direct evidence for what each OEM labels
  with a given MY and for its own history narrative. They are not independent
  confirmation, may change dynamically, and do not establish wholesale
  U.S.-market availability.
- **Existing classic supplement provenance:** The catalog source registry
  attributes the pre-1978 selective Ford/Lincoln model-year identities to
  Wikipedia contributors (CC BY-SA 4.0). That is not a publisher site used in
  this research pass, and those records still need archival/OEM corroboration
  before treating historical coverage as independently verified.
- More than five distinct source URLs are retained across fetched documents
  and search evidence. Search-only results are marked as leads. The fetched
  evidence paths are:
  - `research/sources/ford-lincoln-04-wwii-production.md`
  - `research/sources/ford-lincoln-05-henry-ford-production-records.md`
  - `research/sources/ford-lincoln-06-lincoln-2026-2027.md`
  - `research/sources/ford-lincoln-search-snippets.md`
  - Existing supporting files: `research/sources/ford-lincoln-01-wartime-production.md`
    and `research/sources/ford-lincoln-02-lincoln-town-car.md`.
- Only short search-result excerpts were preserved where required; no source
  prose, tables, or images were copied into this report. Nothing here is an
  approval of catalog reuse or production release.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions

- The API census is reconciled. This focused report is not an independent
  source-by-source verification of each Ford/Lincoln historical fact: the EPA
  and annual-guide source layers were matched, while the selected secondary
  records still need archival corroboration, unresolved gaps need case-by-case
  review, and use rights remain a separate question.
- The 43 detailed trim/configuration profiles are listed separately from the
  4,672 model-year identities. None of those broader profiles establishes
  complete U.S. sales history, and the generation labels remain candidates
  rather than exact configuration evidence.
- Pre-1984 coverage is demonstrably selective. The bundle lacks broad,
  source-verified Ford coverage for 1932–54 and broad Lincoln coverage before
  1940; other blank years/models may reflect product history, source scope,
  naming, or ingestion gaps. None should be silently filled.
- The older Ford/Lincoln profile years in the classic supplement are
  normalized from Wikipedia-attributed source records; year-by-year
  corroboration in surviving production cards, dated sales literature, or
  reliable independent archives remains incomplete.
- Further independent primary/archival documentation is needed for Ford and
  Lincoln model-year introduction/retirement dates, distinct variant/name
  changes, and each 1943–45 civilian-production exception or resumed model
  year. The fetched Henry Ford page notes incomplete runs.
- Obtain dated U.S. model-year order guides/official announcements for the
  exact Ford and Lincoln 2026/2027 identities and confirm if 2027 records are
  preliminary. Dynamic live model pages are evidence only for the retrieval
  date.
- Check source-use rights for any commercial retention, normalized derivative,
  and public display separately. This work does not itself clear rights.
- No public release until the owner reviews and explicitly approves it.
