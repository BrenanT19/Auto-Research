name: Publisher source access terms
description: Independent publication terms that restrict automated retrieval during automotive research.
---

PR Newswire's Terms of Use prohibit scraping, robots, spiders, automated retrieval, and republication of site content without prior permission. G/O Media's Terms of Service also prohibit automated systems that access its services, except standard search-engine or browser usage. Do not use a mirrored press release or independent article for automated extraction when its own terms prohibit that access.

Volo Auto Sales article pages link to Speed Digital's website terms, which prohibit robots, spiders, search/retrieval applications, or other manual or automatic processes used to retrieve, index, data-mine, or reproduce site content. Treat Volo articles as incompatible with automated research unless written permission or a compatible source is verified.

For the Chrysler Pacifica history batch, the Wikipedia crossover and minivan articles provide a compatible CC BY-SA 4.0 source. Use normalized facts, link the cited article, and attribute Wikipedia contributors; do not copy article prose or reuse embedded images without checking each image's separate license.

**Why:** Independent publishers and site operators can impose access restrictions even when facts are publicly visible. The linked Speed Digital terms are broader than a copyright notice and apply to automated collection from pages that surface them.

**How to apply:** Check each publication's access and reuse terms separately before automated retrieval. Keep source access rights distinct from the copyright status of normalized facts, and use a compatible source rather than assuming that a publicly visible page permits automated collection. Do not use or retain Volo article captures without resolving its linked terms.

Cars.com's Terms of Service prohibit using automated mechanisms—including robots, crawlers, or spiders—to access, query, collect, or scrape site information. If discovered after a capture, exclude the page content from evidence and do not retain it.

**Why:** A Cars.com Mustang research page was fetched before its indexed terms were reviewed; the page is now excluded from the audit, and its facts are not used.

**How to apply:** Do not automate retrieval of Cars.com research pages. Treat its explicit restriction as source-specific; use an independently checked compatible source or leave the fact unresolved.

MotorTrend Group's Terms of Use prohibit robots, crawlers, and other automatic devices or processes without written permission, and separately prohibit automated data downloads. Hearst's U.S. magazine terms bar robots, crawlers, and data-gathering tools from accessing or extracting Hearst content; Car and Driver links to those terms.

**Why:** Search results surfaced explicit access restrictions for both publishers during Mustang research. Their article captures cannot support the audit after those restrictions are known.

**How to apply:** Do not automate retrieval or use saved article captures from these publishers as evidence. Replace each factual claim with a compatible source or mark it unverified; preserve only the minimum metadata needed to record the exclusion.

FCA Website Terms of Use cover Chrysler.com and prohibit robots, spiders, scrapers, or other automated systems from extracting site data. Exclude Chrysler.com history or vehicle-page claims fetched before this restriction is discovered; do not retain page captures as evidence.

**Why:** A Chrysler history page was fetched before reviewing FCA's terms and then had to be excluded from the New Yorker model-year check.

**How to apply:** Check FCA terms before automated retrieval from Chrysler.com. If a page was already fetched, keep only minimal exclusion metadata, discard its factual content from the audit, and seek a source with compatible access terms.
